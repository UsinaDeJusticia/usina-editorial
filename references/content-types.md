# Editorial modes and content forms

Use this reference to decide **how to write the piece**. Do not use it to decide the WordPress category.

## Core rule: category is not tone

The six WordPress categories are taxonomy/navigation metadata. They do **not** define voice, tone, length, structure, research depth, or emotional register.

A piece categorized as `Incidencia` can be a short factual update, a pedagogical explainer, a firm institutional statement, or a data-supported analysis. A `Prensa` piece can be concise and informational or human-centered and reflective. An `Institucional` piece can be celebratory, explanatory, sober, or commemorative.

Never write from the category outward. Write from the **event, evidence, editorial purpose, and audience need** outward; assign the category separately afterward using `site-contract.md`.

## Decision order

Before drafting, determine these dimensions independently:

1. **What happened?** Identify the primary event or fact that makes the publication newsworthy now.
2. **What must the publication accomplish?** Inform, explain, document an action, tell a human story, communicate a position, contextualize a media intervention, analyze evidence, commemorate, or provide practical guidance.
3. **What evidence is available?** Short text, primary documents, interview/video, media links, images, dataset/report, testimony, or mixed sources.
4. **What level of sensitivity applies?** Crime/victimization, minors, privacy, pending litigation, institutional controversy, personal testimony, or ordinary organizational news.
5. **What tone and structure best serve the material?** Choose from the modes below; combine them when necessary.
6. **Only after those decisions, assign the CMS category.** Category selection must never retroactively force a different tone.

## Base editorial voice

Unless the material clearly calls for a modifier, write in a voice that is:

- clear and natural Argentine Spanish;
- precise and fact-led;
- sober without sounding bureaucratic;
- human without becoming sentimental or sensational;
- institutionally credible;
- explicit about victims and harm when crime is involved;
- legally careful about procedural status and contested facts.

The victims-first doctrine is an ethical/editorial orientation, **not a single tone template**. A statistical note can remain analytical; a family story can be compassionate; a legal filing can be firm; a memorial can be reflective. All can still be victim-centered.

## Editorial modes

### 1. Informative / news update

Use when the main value is telling readers what happened now.

Typical shape:
- direct lede with event, actors, and relevance;
- essential context only;
- concrete outcome, next step, or current status.

Keep it short when the event is simple. Do not manufacture sections or background merely to make the note look substantial.

### 2. Explanatory / pedagogical

Use when readers need help understanding a legal rule, institutional mechanism, policy issue, procedural development, or complex background.

Typical shape:
- plain-language lede;
- explain the problem or concept before technical detail;
- translate legal/technical material without distorting it;
- use examples, bullets, or H2s only when they improve comprehension.

This mode may appear in `Incidencia`, `Observatorio`, `Prensa`, `Historias`, or another category.

### 3. Firm institutional / position statement

Use when Usina is making, reiterating, or defending a documented institutional position, request, objection, proposal, or demand.

Typical voice:
- clear and assertive;
- evidence-linked;
- no false balance when the institutional position is itself the news;
- no grandstanding, insults, slogans, or unsupported legal conclusions.

Firm does not mean aggressive. Keep legal and factual status exact.

### 4. Human / testimonial

Use when a victim, family, survivor, or lived experience is the editorial center.

Typical voice:
- dignified and empathetic;
- restrained with violent details;
- centered on the victim's life, harm, agency, search for justice, memory, or needs;
- careful with privacy and identifying information.

Do not turn suffering into narrative decoration or use sentimental language unsupported by the source.

### 5. Analytical / evidence-led

Use for datasets, reports, monitoring, trends, comparisons, or evidence-heavy material.

Typical shape:
- strongest supported finding first;
- period, geography, source, methodology, and denominator where needed;
- interpretation separated from observation;
- limitations stated when material;
- no dramatic extrapolation from incomplete data.

The conclusion can connect findings to victims or policy implications without converting analysis into advocacy unsupported by the data.

### 6. Media-contextual

Use when the event is an interview, article, radio/TV appearance, podcast, stream, or public intervention through a media outlet.

Typical shape:
- identify participant, outlet/program, and substantive topic;
- extract the meaningful position or argument rather than summarize minute by minute;
- add enough context to make the intervention understandable;
- treat the outlet's framing as separate from verified facts and Usina's own position.

Tone follows the substance of the intervention, not the `Prensa` category label.

### 7. Practical / accompaniment-oriented

Use when the publication needs to explain support, guidance, a process, available help, or concrete steps relevant to victims or families.

Typical voice:
- direct, useful, reassuring without making promises the organization cannot guarantee;
- explain what Usina did or what a person needs to know;
- avoid unnecessary procedural jargon and private details.

### 8. Institutional announcement / milestone

Use for events, partnerships, appointments, recognitions, launches, governance, milestones, or ordinary organizational news.

Typical voice depends on the event:
- concise and factual for routine announcements;
- warmer for celebrations or recognitions;
- reflective for anniversaries or memorial contexts;
- explanatory when a partnership or initiative needs context.

Do not inflate routine news into a manifesto or legal essay.

### 9. Commemorative / remembrance

Use when the publication marks an anniversary, remembrance, tribute, or significant date connected to a victim, family, or institutional history.

Typical voice:
- respectful and reflective;
- precise about the facts and date;
- avoid clichés, melodrama, or graphic repetition of the crime;
- foreground memory, dignity, justice, or continuing significance when supported.

### 10. Mixed mode

Many strong pieces combine modes. Examples:

- a petition may be **informative + explanatory + firm institutional**;
- a victim case update may be **informative + human**;
- an interview may be **media-contextual + explanatory**;
- a report launch may be **analytical + institutional announcement**;
- an accompaniment activity may be **informative + practical + human**.

Choose the minimum combination needed. Do not try to make every article use all modes.

## Structure follows complexity, not category

Possible structures include:

- **very short update:** title + 2-5 paragraphs, no internal headings;
- **developed news article:** lede + a few paragraphs + 1-3 meaningful H2 sections;
- **explainer:** lede + concept/problem + explanation + implications;
- **human story:** narrative flow with few or no H2s unless chronology/clarity benefits;
- **analysis/report:** finding + evidence/method + interpretation + limitations;
- **statement:** concise position + factual basis + requested/expected action;
- **media recap:** event + key intervention + necessary context + original link when publishable.

Never impose a minimum heading count or word count.

## Category selection is a separate final step

After the article's editorial mode, tone, and structure are settled, use `site-contract.md` to assign the most appropriate WordPress category based on the **primary editorial object**:

- the human story;
- accompaniment/support action;
- an effort to influence institutions or public policy;
- the media appearance itself;
- organizational/institutional news;
- data/report/monitoring work.

If category is genuinely ambiguous, ask only if the choice affects publishing. Otherwise infer it silently.

## Independence examples

- A formal petition can be `Incidencia` with an **informative-explanatory** tone.
- A public demand to change a policy can be `Incidencia` with a **firm institutional** tone.
- A technical proposal for reform can be `Incidencia` with an **analytical-pedagogical** tone.
- A television interview about a murdered victim can be `Prensa` while the prose remains **human and victim-centered**.
- A statistical report can be `Observatorio` with an **analytical** tone even if its implications support advocacy.
- An anniversary event can be `Institucional` with a **commemorative** tone.

The category tells WordPress **where the piece belongs**. The editorial mode tells the writer **how this particular piece should be written**.
