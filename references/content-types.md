# Adaptive content types

Classify the material by its primary editorial purpose. These are patterns, not mandatory templates. Use only the sections that improve the actual article.

## 1. Incidencia legal or public-policy action

Typical inputs:
- petitions;
- formal requests;
- legislative proposals;
- court or administrative filings;
- institutional letters;
- policy advocacy.

Usually useful:
- self-sufficient lede explaining the action and recipient;
- problem or institutional context;
- relevant legal/factual basis;
- what Usina requested, achieved, or proposed;
- why the action matters for victims;
- supporting documents at the end.

Do not reproduce the filing paragraph by paragraph. Extract its substance.

## 2. Prensa / interview / media appearance

Typical inputs:
- TV, radio, podcast, newspaper, or streaming appearance;
- link to an interview;
- article quoting a member of Usina.

Usually useful:
- what program/outlet and who participated;
- topic that motivated the appearance;
- most substantive positions or statements;
- necessary case or policy context;
- link/embed to original appearance.

Do not summarize every minute. Do not inherit the outlet's framing. Center the substance of Usina's intervention and victims when crime is discussed.

## 3. Historia de víctima o familia

Typical inputs:
- family testimony;
- case chronology;
- remembrance or anniversary;
- account of a justice process.

Usually useful:
- human-centered opening;
- accurate chronology only as needed;
- impact on victim/family;
- search for truth, justice, support, or institutional response;
- current status when verified.

Avoid sensational detail, voyeuristic crime description, victim blaming, or unnecessary identification of private data. Preserve dignity and agency.

## 4. Acompañamiento

Typical inputs:
- support action;
- meeting with a family;
- legal/institutional guidance;
- accompaniment before an agency or hearing;
- resources for victims.

Usually useful:
- who was accompanied, subject to privacy constraints;
- what need or institutional process was involved;
- what Usina did;
- what remains pending;
- practical information when it benefits other victims.

Do not disclose private victim information merely to make the article more concrete.

## 5. Observatorio / data / report

Typical inputs:
- spreadsheet;
- statistical report;
- monitoring dataset;
- homicide/femicide or criminal-justice indicators;
- methodological document.

Usually useful:
- principal finding first;
- source, period, geography, and methodology;
- a small number of meaningful figures;
- interpretation separated from raw results;
- limitations or missing data when material;
- link to dataset/report when publishable.

Never manufacture precision from incomplete data. Explain denominators and comparison periods where necessary.

## 6. Institucional

Typical inputs:
- awards;
- partnerships;
- organizational milestones;
- events;
- governance or team announcements;
- formal statements not primarily about policy advocacy.

Usually useful:
- the event or announcement;
- participants/entities;
- relevance to Usina's mission;
- next step if there is one.

Keep simple institutional news simple. Do not inflate a small announcement into a legal essay.

## 7. Mixed or ambiguous material

Choose the category and structure according to the **primary event readers need to understand**.

Examples:
- A TV interview about a petition already filed: `Prensa` if the news is the interview; `Incidencia` if the interview is merely supporting material for the petition.
- A family's case used inside a statistical report: `Observatorio` if the report is the main publication; `Historias` if the family's experience is the editorial center.

When classification genuinely affects meaning and cannot be inferred, ask one concise question. Otherwise choose and proceed.
