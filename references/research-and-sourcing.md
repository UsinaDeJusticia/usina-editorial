# Research and sourcing

Research is a tool for enrichment and verification, not a ritual. Do not browse merely because a link or PDF exists.

## When to research outside the supplied material

Research when one or more of these conditions apply:

- a supplied link is necessary to understand the event;
- the user's material assumes context a normal reader will not have;
- a current officeholder, institution name, legal status, case status, statistic, or rule needs verification;
- a media report makes a material claim that should be checked against a stronger source;
- the article would materially improve with a primary source such as a law, ruling, official resolution, public dataset, or original interview;
- supplied sources conflict;
- the user explicitly asks to investigate, verify, or expand.

Research little or not at all when the supplied primary documents already contain enough reliable substance.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order when relevant:

1. documents supplied by Usina that are authentic primary materials;
2. court decisions, legislation, official gazettes, prosecutors, public agencies, official datasets, and other primary institutional sources;
3. original interviews, speeches, videos, transcripts, and direct statements;
4. reputable journalism with direct reporting;
5. secondary commentary and analysis;
6. social posts and aggregators mainly as leads unless they are the first-party source of the statement/event.

A lower-ranked source can still be useful, but do not let it silently override stronger evidence.

## Media framing filter

For each journalistic source, mentally separate:

- verified factual claims;
- direct quotations or clearly attributed statements;
- interpretation;
- speculation;
- narrative framing.

Use the first two when supported. Verify material facts where practical. Do not import interpretive framing that conflicts with Usina's victim-centered mission.

Examples of problematic framing include language that:

- turns an offender into the emotional protagonist without editorial necessity;
- treats serious violence as a vague "episode" when the established fact is more specific;
- presents criminal conduct as inevitable, glamorous, admirable, or excusable;
- foregrounds the offender's aspirations or hardships while erasing the victim's harm;
- speculates about victim behavior to dilute responsibility.

Context about an accused/offender may be included when necessary and verified, but state it neutrally and do not convert context into justification.

## Legal and factual precision

Never upgrade legal status through prose.

Use the status supported by the strongest current source:

- suspected / sought;
- under investigation;
- charged / formally accused;
- on trial;
- convicted;
- final conviction when relevant;
- perpetrator/author only when authorship is established or when quoting/attributing a source that uses that characterization.

Where a case remains contested, attribute claims precisely.

## Privacy and safety

Before publishing victim information, consider whether names, addresses, contact details, health details, minors' identities, exact locations, or other sensitive information are actually necessary and already public/authorized.

A primary document can contain information that should not automatically be republished on the open web.

If disclosure creates a plausible privacy or safety issue, omit the unnecessary detail and surface an `ALERTA EDITORIAL`.

## Links in the finished article

Link to primary or authoritative sources when the link genuinely helps readers verify or understand the claim. Do not clutter the article with a bibliography of everything consulted.

When the user's attachment is the documentary basis of the article, list it under supporting documents/links rather than forcing repeated citations throughout the prose unless a particular claim benefits from an inline link.
